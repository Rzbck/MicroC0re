# Vision

MicroC0re is an artificial microscopic world intended to be watched rather than “played” in the conventional sense.

The desired experience is closer to looking through a fictional laboratory microscope: organisms drift, search, collide, starve, divide, form temporary structures, exploit gradients, alter their environment, and eventually participate in predator/prey and symbiotic interactions.

## What should make it beautiful

The visual complexity should come from three layers interacting:

1. **Continuous chemistry** — nutrient plumes, waste, signals, toxins, morphogens.
2. **Discrete organisms** — cells with geometry, metabolism, motility, lineage, and local rules.
3. **Pixel microscope rendering** — a deliberately low-resolution observation layer with palettes, dithering, temporal texture, and readable silhouettes.

The renderer may stylize the world heavily. The underlying state should remain continuous and independent from the pixel grid.

## Long-term behaviors

Potential later systems include:
- bacterial colonies and branching fronts;
- biofilm-like adhesion and extracellular matrix;
- predatory bacteria;
- amoeboid/protist predators with visible engulfment;
- lysis and nutrient recycling;
- quorum-like signaling;
- pili / conjugation and genetic exchange;
- mutation and inheritable traits;
- dormancy/spore-like states where appropriate;
- multiple chemical niches;
- reaction-diffusion morphogenesis;
- artificial-life fusion rules, clearly marked when not biologically literal.

## Principle

We want emergence, not choreography.

An organism should not be told “go to food, split every 10 seconds, die when touched.” It should sense local information, exchange matter/energy with its environment, and change state because the equations and thresholds make that outcome possible.
